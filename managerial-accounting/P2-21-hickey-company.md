## Cost behavior summary (supporting analysis)

With respect to **units produced**, and assuming direct labor varies with
production:

| Cost | Behavior | Variable rate | Fixed amount |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Direct materials used | Variable | $5.667/unit | — |
| Direct labor | Variable | $2.667/unit | — |
| Royalty on patent | Variable | $1.00/unit | — |
| Special equipment rent | Mixed | $0.10/unit | $6,000 |
| Indirect labor | Fixed | — | $60,000 |
| Building rent — factory share | Fixed | — | $40,000 |
| Utilities, factory | Fixed (treated as such here) | — | $35,000 |
| Maintenance, factory | Fixed | — | $25,000 |
| Other factory overhead | Fixed | — | $11,000 |
| **Totals** | | **$9.433/unit** | **$177,000** |

Check: (30,000 × $9.4333) + $177,000 = $283,000 + $177,000 = $460,000 = total
manufacturing costs, as above.

Because $177,000 of the manufacturing cost is fixed, the **average** cost per
unit falls as volume rises — e.g. at 40,000 units the average would be
$9.433 + ($177,000 ÷ 40,000) = $13.86 rather than $15.00 — even though total
fixed cost and the variable rate per unit are both unchanged. Unit product cost
is therefore only valid at the volume for which it was computed.

## Note on the source figures

On the scanned page the "Sales in units" and "Ending finished goods inventory in
units" lines are partly covered by handwriting. This solution reads them as
26,000 units sold and 4,000 units in ending inventory (the latter being the
"?" the problem asks you to compute). The reading is self-consistent: it
produces a $15.00 unit product cost, a $25.00 selling price, and a $40,000 net
operating income, all exact.
